Five stories I can go deep on

Filling a room with 36 senior product marketers, with no events team and no paid promotion. Ask me how you source a panel from Atlassian, Meta Reality Labs, and Google without buying a single speaker. The short version: the room is the product, and the panel was a payoff of a partner network built months earlier. The full write-up is here.

Building $400K in qualified pipeline from a standing start. No brand, no list, no inbound, no sales development rep. Ask me how 134 accounts got found, researched, reached through a six-week sequence of 1,000 outbound touches, and tracked by stage in HubSpot. Ask me what "qualified" means here, because it is not booked revenue and I will not pretend it is.

Turning 27 design partners into proof, panelists, and the first enterprise deal. Questions I expect you to ask

And the honest answers, including the uncomfortable ones

"In all of this, what was yours versus the team's?" At the pre-seed stage there was no team to share it with. I owned the ICP, the named-account list, the outbound, the event, the design partner program, and the reporting as one person holding every thread. Where a number reflects a whole company's revenue rather than my own work, such as the POLITICO figures, I say so before you ask.

"What would you do differently?" I would instrument attribution earlier. The $400K pipeline is real and tracked, but I built the funnel instrumentation after the outbound motion was already running, so I cannot draw a perfectly clean line from the field event to specific closed revenue. Next time, the tracking goes in before the first touch, not after.

"Where are you weak?" My depth is in early and mid-stage motions built by a small number of people. I have not managed a large paid-media budget or led a big marketing team, and I will not claim I have. My strength is finding the buyer, building the motion that reaches them, and proving whether it actually drove pipeline.

"Can you actually build the systems, or do you just manage them?" I build them. The lifecycle automation runs across n8n, HubSpot, and Calendly triggers I wired myself. I queried drop-off in SQL to map the abandonment stages, and I directed a Salesforce and OMS migration with automated validation across more than 26,000 deal records. Ask me to whiteboard any of it.

"Why should a room of skeptical buyers, or skeptical interviewers, believe your numbers?" Because I tell you what each one does not mean. A number only matters when it says what it does and does not claim. That habit is the whole reason this page exists.

What I am looking for

So we can both tell if this is a fit

A motion I can own end to end, at least early. I do my best work when one person holds every thread: positioning, reach, conversion, and the proof. That coherence is worth more than the efficiency of splitting it up. I can systematize and hand off later; the first version I want to build myself.

A team that respects evidence over narrative. I would rather bring you a smaller number I can defend than a bigger one I would have to walk back. If that is how your team argues, we will get along.

A real buyer worth finding. The work I am proudest of started with a hard audience that ignored vendor outreach. Point me at a buyer who is difficult to reach and I will build the motion that reaches them.
